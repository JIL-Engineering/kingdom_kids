# Kingdom Kids — Content Pipeline: A Framework for JIL Engineering

*This isn't a finished plan to execute — it's the structure and the open questions. JIL Engineering and CMFI's content/ministry side need to fill in the real answers together.*

## Why this piece needs direct leadership involvement

Everything else in this project is technical risk the engineering team can absorb internally. This one isn't — it depends on people at CMFI outside JIL Engineering's control (who has the original books, who can approve theological content, who can narrate in French). Engineering can design the pipeline; only leadership-level conversations with CMFI make it real.

## The pipeline, as a sequence of decisions to make

**1. Source inventory** — Someone needs to go through CMFI's existing published children's material and determine: how many titles exist, in what format (print only? Word docs? old PDFs?), who holds the rights/originals, and which 15–25 are the strongest candidates for a Phase 1 launch. This requires someone at CMFI with institutional knowledge — identifying that person is the first concrete assignment to hand out.

**2. Digitization workflow** — For each chosen book: transcribe/clean the text per page, decide whether existing illustrations can be rescanned/reused or need redrawing, and translate into French if the original is English-only (or vice versa). Open question: does the team have image cleanup/rescanning capability internally, or does that need a hired designer? This connects directly to the design/UX brief — worth sequencing that conversation alongside this one.

**3. Narration** — Who records English and French audio? Existing CMFI staff/volunteers with strong voices may be a good starting point before commissioning professional voice talent. Question for CMFI leadership: is there already a "voice of CMFI" they'd want representing the ministry, or should this be auditioned fresh?

**4. Theological review** — Every piece of text needs a sign-off step before it goes live. Who at CMFI has that authority, and what does their review process look like — a checklist, a read-through, a formal approval? This needs to be a real, named step in the workflow, not an assumption that "someone will check it."

**5. Data entry into the pipeline** — The one piece that's genuinely an engineering deliverable: the Google Sheet template + import script from the technical spec. Once the content team has clean text, translated text, and audio/image files ready, this is where it becomes structured data in the database. Build target: Sprint 2.

## Open questions to resolve before this doc is finalized

- Who is the content/theology point of contact at CMFI?
- Roughly how many existing books are realistically usable for Phase 1?
- Is there existing capacity to narrate in French, or is that a gap to fill?
- Is there an existing illustration style CMFI wants to preserve, or is a fresh visual direction on the table? (feeds directly into the design/UX brief)

Once these are answered, this becomes a concrete pipeline document with real names, real timelines, and a real title list.
