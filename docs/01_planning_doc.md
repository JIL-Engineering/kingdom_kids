# Kingdom Kids — Technical & Business Planning Document

*Prepared for JIL Engineering — August 2026*

## 1. The Core Challenge

The vision document describes a full ecosystem: library, narration, bilingual support, gamification, devotionals, Scripture memorization, Sunday school tools, parent dashboard, DRM, offline access, and eventually animation, games, music, and AI storytelling. Built all at once, this is a multi-year, large-team effort. Built in the right order, a small team can ship something real in a few months and grow it into the full vision over time.

The plan below breaks the vision into three phases, proposes a concrete technical architecture, and lays out the team, cost, and content realities of actually delivering this as a product for CMFI's children's ministry.

---

## 2. Phased Roadmap

### Phase 1 — MVP (target: 3–4 months)

Goal: prove the core loop — a child opens the app, reads/listens to a real CMFI book, and a parent sees it happened — with enough polish that it feels like a "vibrant world," not a PDF viewer.

- Parent account creation; child sub-profiles under each parent account (age, name, preferred language — no direct personal data collection from the child)
- Digitized library of CMFI's *existing* published children's books (start with 15–25 titles, not new content — see Section 4)
- Interactive reader: page-turn animation, illustrations, adaptive text size by age
- Read-aloud narration synced to text, in English and French
- Bilingual UI and content (English/French from day one, architected so more languages are just "add a language row," not a rebuild)
- Basic gamification: reading streaks, "first book," "five stories" badges — simple rule-based, not a full XP economy yet
- Daily verse + short devotional thought, with a push notification reminder
- Offline download of individual books
- Parent dashboard: books completed, time spent, streaks
- Basic child-safety baseline: no ads, PIN-protected parent controls, encrypted storage, no public social features

Explicitly deferred to later phases: Scripture memorization *games* (a static daily verse is enough for MVP), Sunday school/teacher tools, full DRM/watermarking, animated characters, coloring, music, podcasts, AI storytelling.

### Phase 2 — Discipleship & Classroom Layer (6–9 months after MVP)

- Scripture memorization games: flashcards, word-matching, fill-in-the-blank
- Sunday school integration: church/organization accounts, teacher role, classes, assigned reading lists and devotional plans, per-student progress view
- Fuller gamification: XP, levels, "Bible Explorer" tiers
- Content protection hardening: encrypted downloads, license-tied playback, subtle watermarking
- Third and fourth languages, based on where CMFI is actually planting this

### Phase 3 — Full Ecosystem (ongoing, 12+ months out)

- Animated Bible stories / Christian cartoons
- Interactive Bible games and coloring activities
- Worship music and Christian podcasts
- Bible quizzes, virtual discipleship classrooms
- AI-powered storytelling (this is the highest-risk, most expensive item on the whole list — theologically sensitive content generated by AI needs a human review layer no matter how good the model is; treat it as a distinct, later project, not a Phase 1–2 assumption)

## 3. Technical Architecture

### Stack recommendation

**Frontend:** Flutter — one codebase for iOS, Android, and (later) tablet-optimized layouts, which matter a lot for a picture-book reading app.

**Backend:** Serverpod (Dart), on PostgreSQL. See the separate Backend Decision document for the full rationale and trade-offs, including how content management is handled without a built-in admin panel.

**Content model:** Don't store books as PDFs or flat images. Model each book as structured data — a `Book` with an ordered list of `Page` objects, each holding text, an illustration reference, and an audio clip reference, all keyed by `book_id + language`. This is what makes bilingual support, adaptive typography, and future animation/interactivity possible without redoing the content pipeline later.

**Media storage:** Cloudflare R2 behind a CDN for illustrations and audio — this avoids the egress fees that get expensive fast with a global, media-heavy children's app.

**Offline & sync:** Downloaded book bundles cached locally (Hive/SQLite in Flutter), synced against a backend "what's new" endpoint. Reading progress and gamification events queue locally and sync when connectivity returns — essential given the explicit low-connectivity use case (villages, church camps).

**Child data & auth:** Parents authenticate normally; children are sub-profiles with no independent login credentials or personal data collection. Treat this like COPPA/GDPR-K compliance even outside jurisdictions that require it — it's the right default for a children's ministry product and it's much easier to build in from the start than retrofit.

**Content protection (realistic version):** Full enterprise DRM (à la Adobe) is expensive and mostly unnecessary at current scale. A pragmatic version: encrypted local storage for downloaded content, short-lived signed URLs for streamed content, and light watermarking (e.g., embedding the licensed parent account ID unobtrusively) so leaked content is traceable. Save true DRM infrastructure for if/when piracy becomes an actual observed problem, not a hypothetical one.

**Sunday school / multi-tenancy (Phase 2):** Requires a real data model change — Church/Organization → Class → Student, with a Teacher role scoped to their own classes. Worth designing the schema early even if the UI isn't built until Phase 2, to avoid migrating live data later.

**Notifications:** Firebase Cloud Messaging for devotional and streak reminders.

**Localization:** Standard Flutter `intl` for UI strings; content localization lives in the `Book`/`Page` data model itself, not the app bundle — so adding French content doesn't require an app release.

## 4. Content Reality Check

The vision document mentions CMFI has "years of ministry, writing, editing, and publishing" behind it. That's the single biggest asset for this project and the thing that should shape Phase 1 scope: **the first priority is digitizing and structuring existing CMFI books, not commissioning new ones.** Selecting 15–25 of the strongest existing titles, getting them into the structured book format above, and narrating them in English and French produces a real MVP library without a content-creation bottleneck.

New content creation, illustration commissioning, and expanded narration become an ongoing Phase 2+ pipeline once the platform itself is proven.

## 5. Team & Roles

Roles JIL Engineering needs to staff or fill for this project:

- **Flutter developer(s)** and **backend/Serverpod developer(s)** — core product engineering.
- **UI/UX + illustration direction** — this is not optional for a kids' app; the visual bar for "vibrant, animated-feeling world" is high, and it's the single easiest place for the product to feel cheap if under-resourced.
- **Content/theology reviewer (CMFI-side)** — approves every book, devotional, and verse selection before it ships. This role exists whether or not a formal "content team" is stood up.
- **Narrators** — bilingual voice talent (English/French); can start small (even 1–2 people) and scale.
- **Translator** — for French content and any future languages; doesn't need to be full-time at MVP scale.
- **CMFI-side product owner** — someone in ministry leadership who prioritizes which books go in first, signs off on Sunday school workflow assumptions, and is the bridge between what churches actually need and what gets built.

A small core team can realistically own Flutter + Serverpod + DevOps for Phase 1. Phase 2's Sunday school/multi-tenant work is a natural point to bring in additional engineering capacity if the team hasn't grown by then.

## 6. Rough Cost & Timeline

**Timeline:** MVP in 3–4 months is realistic for a small, focused team.

**Ongoing infrastructure costs at MVP scale:** effectively zero — Postgres hosted free-tier on Supabase, R2/CDN storage within its free 10GB/zero-egress tier at MVP content volume, Firebase free tier for notifications, and Serverpod Cloud's entry tier for temporary hosting during build and pilot. Real cost concentrates entirely in people — illustration, narration, translation — not infrastructure, at this stage. Paid infrastructure tiers are a "revisit when usage data shows it's needed" decision, not a Phase 1 line item.

**One-time:** Apple Developer Program ($99/year), Google Play developer account ($25 one-time).

## 7. Funding & Sustainability Model

This is a ministry tool, not a consumer SaaS product, and that should drive the model rather than be an afterthought bolted on later. A few options worth CMFI leadership discussing directly with JIL Engineering, not mutually exclusive:

- **Free for families**, funded through CMFI's ministry budget or earmarked donor/grant funding for children's ministry technology — keeps the mission-alignment clean.
- **Institutional licensing for churches/Sunday schools** — the teacher/classroom tools (Phase 2) are the natural place for a small annual fee from partner churches, while family use stays free.
- **"Sponsor a child's access"** — a donation mechanic tied to the app itself, letting supporters fund reach in low-connectivity or lower-income regions.
- **Optional premium content packs** later, if CMFI is comfortable with any monetization — this is the option most in tension with "ministry-first" framing and worth deciding deliberately rather than defaulting into.

---

## 8. Recommended Next Step

Before building the full Phase 1 feature set, run a small pilot: digitize 3–5 books, build just the reader + narration + one parent profile, and test it with one or two partner churches or Sunday school classes. This validates the content pipeline and the reading experience with real families before investing in gamification, devotionals, and the full library — and gives CMFI leadership something concrete to see and approve before greenlighting the larger build.

Success metrics for this pilot should be defined before it starts, not judged after the fact informally — day-7 return rate, minimum average weekly reading time per child, and a qualitative feedback score from participating teachers and parents are the proposed starting metrics; see the sprint plan for where these are finalized.

## 9. Compliance Note

Kingdom Kids is a child-directed app, which brings specific, non-optional obligations beyond general good practice: explicit parental consent before any child profile is created, analytics configured for child-directed treatment, and gamification mechanics (streaks, badges) reviewed against Google Play Families policy and Apple's Kids Category guidelines before submission. These are addressed in the technical spec and built into the Sprint 8 checklist — flagged here because a compliance failure at app-store review is a launch-blocking risk, not a minor fix.
